# 1.1.4 — Cybersecurity vs. Information Security

## 1. Overview

Cybersecurity and information security are closely related disciplines, but they are not identical.

**Information security** protects information and information systems from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, destruction, or disruption.

**Cybersecurity** manages the risks associated with information, systems, networks, applications, devices, and services that are connected to or depend on cyberspace.

Information security can include both digital and non-digital information, while cybersecurity is primarily concerned with digital environments and the systems that connect, process, or control them.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Information Security

Information security is the practice of protecting information in all relevant forms.

It focuses on preserving:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
- Authenticity
- Accountability
- Privacy
- Compliance
- Traceability

Examples include protecting:

- Paper records
- Spoken or verbally communicated information
- Printed financial reports
- Physical filing systems
- Faxed or mailed information
- Digital documents
- Databases
- Backups
- Information held by third parties

### 2.2 Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the practice of managing cyber risk and protecting digital systems, networks, applications, devices, services, and information from cyber threats.

Its scope includes:

- Endpoints and operating systems
- Networks and communication systems
- Cloud services
- Applications and APIs
- Identity systems
- Internet of Things devices
- Operational technology
- Digital supply chains
- Incident response and recovery

Cybersecurity generally involves information security principles, but it adds a stronger focus on cyber threats, connected systems, attack surfaces, online adversaries, monitoring, incident response, and digital recovery.

## 3. Key Differences

| Area | Information Security | Cybersecurity |
|---|---|---|
| Primary scope | Information in any form | Digital systems and cyber-connected environments |
| Main focus | Protection of information and its systems | Protection from, detection of, and recovery from cyber threats |
| Threat context | Natural, accidental, internal, and external risks | Cyber threat actors and digital attack techniques |
| Typical concerns | Information classification, access, retention, physical storage | Network exposure, malware, identity compromise, vulnerabilities, monitoring |
| Formats covered | Digital and non-digital | Primarily digital |
| Related disciplines | Records management, privacy, physical security, governance | Information security, networking, cloud security, application security, incident response |
| Objective | Preserve information security properties | Manage and reduce cyber risk across interconnected systems |

The exact boundary varies between organizations. In practical use, the terms often overlap significantly.

## 4. Areas of Overlap

Cybersecurity and information security share many objectives and practices.

### Shared concerns

Both disciplines seek to:

- Protect confidentiality
- Preserve integrity
- Maintain availability
- Manage risk
- Control access
- Protect sensitive data
- Define security responsibilities
- Apply policies and standards
- Monitor for security problems
- Respond to incidents
- Support recovery
- Protect information from misuse
- Maintain evidence and audit records

### Shared controls

Examples include:

- Identity and access management
- Least-privilege access
- Encryption
- Data classification
- Security monitoring
- Incident response
- Backup and recovery
- Vendor management
- Employee awareness
- Risk assessments
- Change management
- Security policies
- Audit and assurance

## 5. When Information Security Is the Broader Concept

Information security may be broader when it includes protection of information that is not created or stored digitally.

Examples include:

- A sensitive paper customer file
- Confidential records stored in a locked filing cabinet
- A trade secret discussed in a secure meeting
- A printed product design
- A physical key or access badge used to protect restricted information
- A company document sent through a controlled physical mail system

Cybersecurity may protect the digital systems that process or store this information, but information security also protects the physical forms and human handling of the information.

For example, encrypting an electronic document protects the digital copy, but a printed copy may require secure storage, authorized access, clear desk practices, and controlled destruction.

## 6. When Cybersecurity Is the More Specific or Operational Concept

Cybersecurity is often the more operational discipline when dealing with:

- Network attacks
- Malware
- Exploited vulnerabilities
- Compromised identities
- Phishing
- Ransomware
- Denial-of-service attacks
- Security monitoring
- Threat detection
- Incident containment
- Digital forensics
- Secure software development
- Cloud security
- Internet of Things security
- Operational technology security
- Cyber-physical system security
- Digital supply-chain attacks

A paper-record incident may fall primarily under information security and records management. A compromise of a web application or connected environment is primarily a cybersecurity concern.

## 7. Digital Information Example

Consider a company that maintains employee salary records.

Information security objectives may include:

- Defining who may view salary information
- Protecting printed and electronic records
- Preventing unauthorized disclosure
- Keeping records accurate
- Retaining records according to policy
- Securely destroying obsolete records
- Restricting access to records held by suppliers
- Defining accountability for decisions involving salary data

Cybersecurity objectives may include:

- Securing the salary database
- Preventing unauthorized application access
- Applying least privilege
- Encrypting data in transit and at rest
- Monitoring database activity
- Detecting malicious accounts or unusual access
- Preventing malware or ransomware from affecting the system
- Recovering data after an incident
- Protecting the application from vulnerability exploitation

The information-security controls and cybersecurity controls work together.

## 8. Relationship to Privacy

Information security and cybersecurity help protect information and systems, but privacy has a broader focus on the appropriate use of personal information.

Privacy concerns include:

- Whether personal information is collected lawfully
- Whether collection and use have an appropriate purpose
- Whether users are informed appropriately
- Whether unnecessary information is collected
- Whether personal information is shared with authorized parties
- Whether inaccurate information is corrected
- Whether personal information is retained appropriately
- Whether data subjects can exercise applicable rights

A system can have strong encryption and access controls yet process personal information improperly.

Similarly, a privacy program may have appropriate policies but lack technical controls to prevent unauthorized access.

## 9. Relationship to Physical Security

Information security often includes physical protections, while cybersecurity usually focuses on digital threats.

Examples of physical security controls include:

- Locks and controlled entrances
- Security guards
- Visitor management
- Secure storage
- Access badges
- Surveillance
- Clear desk procedures
- Secure disposal
- Protection of removable media
- Environmental controls

Cybersecurity depends on physical security as well.

For example:

- An attacker who gains physical access may steal or alter a device.
- An authorized user with unrestricted access to a server room may bypass a logical security control.
- A loss of power or environmental control may affect availability.
- A stolen laptop may expose stored data or authenticated sessions.

## 10. Relationship to IT Security

Information security and cybersecurity are not limited to the IT department.

**IT security** is generally the protection of information technology assets and technical environments, including:

- Endpoints
- Servers
- Networks
- Applications
- Identity systems
- Data storage
- Cloud infrastructure
- Security configuration

Cybersecurity includes the technical IT environment while also covering risk, governance, supply chains, people, physical consequences, incident response, and organizational resilience.

Information security provides a broader information-centric framework that may include IT security, records management, privacy, physical security, and governance.

## 11. Practical Comparison

| Scenario | Primary discipline |
|---|---|
| Confidential paper documents left unattended | Information security and physical security |
| Network intrusion into a cloud application | Cybersecurity |
| Loss of a customer database | Cybersecurity and information security |
| Improper sharing of personal information | Privacy and information security |
| Malware on an employee laptop | Cybersecurity |
| A contract that does not define record-retention duties | Information security and governance |
| An unauthorized configuration change | Cybersecurity and information security |
| A physical server stolen from a data center | Cybersecurity and physical security |
| A signed policy requiring secure record disposal | Information security governance |

In practice, an incident may involve multiple disciplines. Organizations do not need to select only one label. The important step is identifying all applicable risks and responsibilities.

## 12. A Unified View

A practical way to understand the relationship is:

- **Information security** is the broader discipline of protecting information and information systems.
- **Cybersecurity** is the connected, technology-focused discipline for managing cyber risks and protecting digital environments.
- **Cybersecurity is generally considered part of information security**, especially in an organizational context.
- **Information security is not limited to cyberspace**, but many information-security controls are implemented through digital systems.
- **Privacy, physical security, records management, IT security, and risk management** contribute to both disciplines.

The two terms should not be used in a way that creates artificial separation. The organization’s actual goals should determine which security objectives and controls are needed.

## 13. Common Misconceptions

### Misconception 1: Cybersecurity only means information security

**Correction:** Cybersecurity includes information protection, but it also addresses connected digital systems, cyber threats, digital response, recovery, and operational consequences.

### Misconception 2: Information security only means cybersecurity

**Correction:** Information security may include physical documents, records, people, meetings, storage, and physical security processes.

### Misconception 3: The terms are completely interchangeable

**Correction:** They overlap significantly, but their scopes and emphasis differ.

### Misconception 4: Protecting digital information is sufficient

**Correction:** Sensitive information may exist in printed records, employee knowledge, conversations, physical devices, or third-party systems.

### Misconception 5: A security team can separate information security from cybersecurity

**Correction:** The work generally overlaps. Roles and organizational terminology may vary, but both disciplines require shared governance and integrated controls.

## 14. Key Takeaways

1. Information security protects information and information systems in all relevant forms.
2. Cybersecurity focuses on managing risks to interconnected digital systems and cyber-dependent operations.
3. The two disciplines share the CIA triad and many security controls.
4. Information security can include physical and non-digital information.
5. Cybersecurity places stronger emphasis on cyber threats, attack surfaces, monitoring, and digital recovery.
6. Privacy, IT security, physical security, governance, and risk management are related disciplines.
7. The disciplines should be applied together rather than treated as isolated functions.
