# 1.1.5 — Cybersecurity vs. IT Security

## 1. Overview

Cybersecurity and IT security are closely related, but they have different scopes.

**IT security** protects information technology assets and systems, including devices, operating systems, networks, applications, identities, and infrastructure.

**Cybersecurity** also protects IT environments, but broader cyber risk, governance, human behavior, supply chains, connected environments, digital threats, incident response, and recovery.

Most IT-security work is cybersecurity work. IT security is therefore generally an important subset of cybersecurity.

## 2. IT Security

IT security is the practice of protecting information technology systems and services against unauthorized access, misuse, modification, interruption, and loss.

It commonly covers:

- End-user devices
- Servers
- Operating systems
- Databases
- Enterprise networks
- Applications
- Email and collaboration systems
- Identity and access systems
- Cloud and virtual infrastructure
- Backups and recovery systems
- IT configuration and patch management
- Monitoring and logging

IT security seeks to maintain confidentiality, integrity, and availability while supporting the organization’s technology services.

## 3. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the broader practice of managing cyber risk across digital and cyber-dependent environments.

It includes:

- IT security
- Identity and access security
- Network security
- Cloud security
- Application security
- Internet of Things security
- Operational technology security
- Cyber-physical system security
- Security governance
- Risk management
- Supply-chain security
- Threat detection
- Incident response
- Digital forensics
- Recovery and resilience
- Security education and awareness

Cybersecurity considers not only technical vulnerabilities, but also people, processes, third parties, operational consequences, legal obligations, and organizational risk.

## 4. Key Differences

| Area | IT Security | Cybersecurity |
|---|---|---|
| Primary scope | Information technology assets | Digital and cyber-dependent environments |
| Main focus | Security of IT systems and services | Management of broader cyber risks |
| Typical assets | Computers, servers, networks, applications | IT assets plus IoT, OT, cloud, and cyber-physical systems |
| Primary disciplines | System hardening, access control, patching, monitoring | Risk management, architecture, governance, threat detection, incident response |
| Organizational scope | Mainly IT operations and technology teams | Organization-wide, including leadership, suppliers, operations, legal, and risk |
| Consequences considered | Technical and information impact | Technical, financial, operational, safety, privacy, regulatory, and reputational impact |
| Relationship | A major part of cybersecurity | A broader discipline that includes IT security |

These boundaries vary between organizations. The terms are often used interchangeably in ordinary conversation.

## 5. Areas of Overlap

IT security and cybersecurity share many activities:

- Managing identities and access
- Applying least privilege
- Secure system configuration
- Vulnerability management
- Patching and updating
- Protecting networks
- Encrypting data
- Monitoring security events
- Managing security logs
- Responding to incidents
- Protecting backups
- Managing security changes
- Training users
- Assessing risks
- Evaluating security controls

An effective cybersecurity program cannot ignore IT security. Most digital incidents involve compromised IT assets or services.

## 6. IT Security in Enterprise Environments

Enterprise IT security commonly addresses:

- Workstation and server security
- Operating-system hardening
- Secure network administration
- Firewall and firewall-policy management
- Endpoint detection and response
- Application and database security
- Email and collaboration security
- Privileged-access management
- Backup and restore operations
- Vulnerability assessments
- Configuration baselines
- Security information and event management

IT security teams commonly use technical controls and operational procedures to protect the enterprise computing environment.

## 7. Cybersecurity Beyond Traditional IT Security

Cybersecurity extends beyond traditional IT systems.

### Cloud environments

Cybersecurity includes cloud identities, configurations, workloads, service interfaces, shared-responsibility boundaries, and provider dependencies.

### Internet of Things

Cybersecurity includes device authentication, firmware integrity, update processes, communication security, and device life-cycle management.

### Operational technology

Cybersecurity includes protection of programmable controllers, industrial networks, physical-process monitoring, and industrial automation.

A traditional IT-security team may be responsible for the office network, while cybersecurity teams coordinate risk across enterprise IT, OT, suppliers, and physical operations.

### Cyber-physical systems

Cybersecurity considers the interaction between digital systems and physical processes, including medical devices, building systems, transportation, and industrial operations.

## 8. Cybersecurity and IT Governance

IT governance determines how an organization manages and directs its technology.

Cybersecurity governance establishes:

- Security objectives
- Risk ownership
- Accountability
- Security policies
- Decision authority
- Investment priorities
- Risk acceptance
- Compliance responsibilities
- Supplier oversight
- Performance measurement
- Incident escalation

Traditional IT operations may execute technical controls, while cybersecurity governance defines risk expectations and ensures that those controls support organizational objectives.

## 9. Cybersecurity and IT Risk

IT risk is a type of risk associated with technology use and failure.

Examples include:

- Server failure
- Software defects
- Configuration errors
- Loss of business data
- Capacity exhaustion
- Insufficient service availability
- Failed patches
- Technology obsolescence

Cyber risk includes IT risk but also considers threats created by malicious or accidental cyber activity, including:

- Account compromise
- Ransomware
- Vulnerability exploitation
- Network intrusion
- Supply-chain compromise
- Manipulation of data or configuration
- Disruption of critical services
- Privacy breaches
- Unauthorized access to industrial systems

IT security aims to make IT reliable and secure. Cybersecurity aims to manage cyber risk, including threats that exploit IT reliability and trust.

## 10. Practical Comparison

| Scenario | Primary discipline |
|---|---|
| A server is missing a critical security patch | IT security |
| An attacker exploits the missing patch to compromise a server | IT security and cybersecurity |
| A business accepts residual risk for a cloud workload | Cybersecurity governance |
| A firewall blocks unauthorized network traffic | IT security |
| A malware incident affects cloud and operational systems | Cybersecurity incident response |
| An IoT device is compromised through weak authentication | Cybersecurity |
| A disaster recovery plan restores a database | IT security and recovery |
| A supplier introduces malware through software updates | Supply-chain cybersecurity |
| A safety process must be protected from unauthorized control changes | OT cybersecurity |
| A help desk resets a compromised user account | IT operations and cybersecurity |

A practical incident often involves both disciplines. Labels do not replace clear ownership and coordinated response.

## 11. Roles and Responsibilities

### IT security responsibilities

IT security teams may:

- Define technical security baselines
- Harden servers and endpoints
- Manage firewalls and network controls
- Monitor IT assets
- Support vulnerability management
- Configure identity controls
- Maintain backups
- Participate in incident response
- Maintain security documentation
- Support compliance activities

### Cybersecurity responsibilities

Cybersecurity teams may:

- Define and communicate the organization’s cyber risk
- Establish policies and standards
- Perform or coordinate threat modelling
- Design security architecture
- Oversee controls across multiple environments
- Coordinate incident response
- Communicate with executives and regulators
- Manage cyber-risk reporting
- Evaluate supplier and third-party risks
- Coordinate recovery and resilience
- Define security metrics
- Support security assurance

In a small organization, the same person may perform both roles.

## 12. Common Misconceptions

### Misconception 1: IT security and cybersecurity are identical

**Correction:** IT security is mainly the protection of information technology systems. Cybersecurity has a broader risk and organizational scope.

### Misconception 2: Cybersecurity is only a function of the IT department

**Correction:** Cybersecurity decisions also involve leadership, engineering, operations, procurement, legal, finance, human resources, and other functions.

### Misconception 3: IT security teams handle every cyber risk

**Correction:** IT teams control many technical risks, but organizational, physical, safety, supplier, and operational risks require broader coordination.

### Misconception 4: Securing the IT environment automatically creates cyber resilience

**Correction:** Security controls are important, but resilience also requires continuity planning, recovery, dependencies, alternatives, and incident coordination.

### Misconception 5: The terms should never be used together

**Correction:** They are commonly used together to describe the relationship between technical IT protection and broader cyber-risk management.

## 13. Key Takeaways

1. IT security protects information technology systems and services.
2. Cybersecurity manages a broader range of cyber risks across digital and cyber-dependent environments.
3. IT security is a major part of cybersecurity.
4. Cybersecurity includes risk management, governance, threat detection, incident response, recovery, and resilience.
5. Cybersecurity extends beyond enterprise IT into cloud, IoT, OT, and cyber-physical systems.
6. IT security and cybersecurity should have clear responsibilities but work together.
7. The distinction is conceptual; real organizations often use the terms interchangeably.
